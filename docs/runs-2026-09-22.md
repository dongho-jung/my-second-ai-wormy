# Where the six-way runs got to, and why movement comes first

Two runs, `a` and `b`, on two nodes of the `apac-jp-stage` cluster. Started
2026-09-21 15:12 UTC on `sha-1956dbd`, six-way self-play, `--patch-scale 4`,
`--torch-threads 8`, two of every six worms an older copy of the policy.

The controlled variable was `--shaping-decay`: `a` faded the aim/approach/explore
ladder over the first three fifths of the run, `b` kept it the whole way.

## Where they got to

At about eleven hours, `a` had 59.98M steps and `b` 57.62M.

| | a (decay 0.6) | b (decay 0) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| combat score | 4.07 | 4.75 |
| bestCombat, last raised at | 55.11M | 49.29M |
| kills / deaths a match | 4.29 / 7.23 | 4.21 / 6.08 |
| damage dealt | 449 | 413 |
| self-damage, share of damage caused | 41% | 35% |
| entropy, share of maximum | 15% | 11% |
| KL per update | 0.012 | 0.014 |
| learning rate | floor | floor |

`bestCombat` climbed the whole way from -4.81 to 4.07 (`a`) and -2.99 to 4.75
(`b`), so the runs did learn. Both stopped: `a` had not raised it for 4.9M steps,
`b` for 8.3M.

## Why they stopped

Over the last 16.5M steps of `a`, a regression on 383 finished-episode samples
put every combat slope inside the noise and only entropy moving:

```
kills       -0.0004 /M   t= -0.09
damage      +0.1049 /M   t=  0.24
K/D         +0.0005 /M   t=  1.39
net damage  +0.9389 /M   t=  1.92
entropy     -0.0200 /M   t=-104.56
self-damage -0.8340 /M   t= -2.21
```

`b` read the same way, with entropy at `t=-74`.

A sharper policy turns the same update into a larger KL, so the `--target-kl`
control kept lowering the rate to hold 0.012, and the rate reached the floor of
`--lr-range`. `b` went past that: at the floor its KL was 0.014, above the
target, with nothing left to lower. The point where each run's rate flattened is
about the point where its `bestCombat` stopped.

The entropy coefficient was scheduled from 1e-4 down to 1e-5 across the run. At
60M steps it was near 7.3e-5, and the entropy term was under 1% of a policy loss
measured at 0.009. `ppo.py` picked those numbers to keep the bonus "near the size
of the thing it is competing with"; against the loss this run actually had, it
was not close.

Note the shape of this: the `--target-kl` control worked. It held the KL where it
was asked to. It did that by spending the whole rate range, and nothing said so
until the rate had been on the floor for hours. That is what
`fix: warn when the rate has nowhere left to go` is for.

## The fix, and what it did

On 2026-09-22 04:23 UTC both runs were restarted from their `best.pt` on
`sha-9322136`, with `--entropy` and `--entropy-final` set to the same value so the
coefficient is constant: `a` 3e-4, `b` 1e-3.

After 38 minutes and about 3M steps:

| | a | b |
| --- | --- | --- |
| entropy | 1.18 → 1.15~1.17 | 0.98 → 0.94~0.97 |
| learning rate | 1.0e-04 | 1.3e-04 |
| KL per update | 0.0155~0.0170 | 0.0136~0.0165 |

Entropy stopped falling and the rate is an order of magnitude off its floor.
Combat numbers are unchanged, which is the expected result over 3M steps and not
evidence either way.

## What this did not fix

Watching the worms play, most of what moves them is recoil and knockback rather
than a decision to go somewhere. Nothing in the metrics contradicts that, because
nothing measures it:

- `stuck` is "moved less than 14px across 60 decisions", four seconds. Being
  shoved further than that is not stuck.
- `exploreMap` pays for entering a grid cell it has not visited. Being shoved
  into one pays the same.

So `CAN IT GET AROUND? 2%` reads well because the bar is low, not because the
worms go where they meant to. There is no term anywhere that asks whether a worm
arrived somewhere it chose.

The reward is built around fighting, and movement is only encouraged through the
ladder terms. None of aim, approach or explore says *where to go*.

## What comes next

Train movement on its own first: a random point on the map, a random destination,
and the worm has to get there. Walking, digging and the rope, nothing else.

The environment already has most of this. `env.js` takes a `goals` setting, calls
`makeGoal(env, agent)` per worm at episode reset, and `progress.js` pays
`goalProgress` per pixel closed and `reachedGoal` on arrival within 24px. What it
does not have is any way for the worm to *see* the goal: there is no destination
in the 212-value vector, in the terrain patch, or in the 32x32 map. Paying for
progress toward a point the policy cannot observe is paying at random.

So the observation gains a goal block, and it stays there for every later stage —
zeros when no goal is set. The action heads do not change. That way the stages
after this one carry the weights over with `--resume` instead of starting again:

1. near goals, same height
2. diagonals and height differences, where jumping and the rope are needed
3. far goals, where digging or a detour is needed
4. fighting again, resuming the movement policy

The cost is that this observation change cannot carry the 60M steps above. The
vector size moves, and `ppo.py` refuses a checkpoint whose layout differs. Those
runs end here, recorded.
